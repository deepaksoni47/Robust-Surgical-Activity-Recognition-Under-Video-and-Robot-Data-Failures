\begin{abstract}
Automated surgical gesture recognition is a cornerstone of context-aware assistance, objective skill assessment, and autonomous execution in robot-assisted minimally invasive surgery (RMIS). While endoscopic video conveys semantic context of tool-tissue interactions, robot kinematics provides continuous physical trajectories immune to optical degradation. However, conventional multimodal models frequently rely on static concatenation or uncalibrated late fusion, leaving them vulnerable when visual corruptions (smoke, fog, blur) or telemetry dropouts occur. In this work, we present an empirical benchmark of an adaptive multimodal framework evaluated across all 103 trials of the JIGSAWS benchmark: Suturing (39 trials), Knot Tying (36 trials), and Needle Passing (28 trials). We combine frozen surgical foundation model features (SurgMotion-ViTL, 1024-D) and ImageNet ResNet-18 embeddings (512-D) with normalized 76-D da Vinci kinematics. Lightweight temporal sequence models (GRU) capture temporal dynamics, while a reliability-aware dynamic gating network calibrates modality confidence from representation statistics and explicit availability flags to enforce exact single-modality fallback under sensor failure. Under official Leave-One-User-Out (LOUO) cross-validation across all active folds (8 folds for Suturing and Knot Tying; 7 active folds for Needle Passing due to an empty test set for Subject G in the official distribution), empirical evaluations demonstrate: (i) Normalized 76-D kinematics provides strong unimodal gesture discrimination ($66.64\%$ accuracy on Suturing, $61.09\%$ on Knot Tying, $48.69\%$ on Needle Passing); (ii) Simple fixed late fusion degrades frame accuracy ($-2.14\%$ to $-12.75\%$) by allowing visual noise to dilute confident kinematics predictions; (iii) Adaptive Reliability Gating achieves the highest accuracy across all three tasks ($72.28\%$ on Suturing, $72.34\%$ on Knot Tying, and $51.48\%$ on Needle Passing), demonstrating a statistically significant $+11.25\%$ gain on Knot Tying ($p=0.0095$, Holm-Bonferroni $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0286$, Cohen's $d_z=1.25$); (iv) Multimodal temporal modeling substantially stabilizes sequence coherence, producing large and statistically significant improvements in segmental Edit Score ($+23.46\%$ to $+34.26\%$, $p < 0.001$, effect sizes $d_z > 2.7$); and (v) Under complete optical telemetry failure, the adaptive gate dynamically routes prediction mass to kinematics, preserving performance with minimal degradation ($\le 0.42\%$ drop on Suturing and Needle Passing).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{R}{obot-assisted} minimally invasive surgery (RMIS) utilizing systems such as the da Vinci Surgical System has fundamentally enhanced surgical dexterity, visualization, and patient recovery trajectories~\cite{gao2014jigsaws, maierhein2022surgical}. Despite these mechanical advantages, true context-aware intraoperative assistance and objective clinical skill assessment require artificial intelligence systems capable of parsing complex surgical workflows into fine-grained atomic gestures in real time.

Surgical activities manifest through two complementary sensing modalities:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Endoscopic Video:} Captures rich visual context, tissue deformation, suture threading, and anatomical morphology. However, optical endoscopy is routinely compromised in operating theaters by lens fog, electrocautery smoke, blood occlusion, rapid camera motion, and digital transmission noise~\cite{rashid2025robustendoclip}.
    \item \textbf{Robot Kinematics:} Continuous time-series of tool-tip Cartesian coordinates, rotation matrices, linear/angular velocities, and gripper closure states. Kinematics is invariant to optical degradation but lacks environmental and tissue contact context~\cite{bai2025grad, zhou2026surgsync}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Research Question \& Hypotheses}
The central question governing this investigation is:
\begin{quote}
\textit{On JIGSAWS surgical gesture recognition, does lightweight temporal fusion of pretrained video representations and robot kinematics outperform unimodal baselines, and does reliability-aware dynamic gating reduce performance loss when one modality is degraded or unavailable?}
\end{quote}

We evaluate five specific hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{H1 (Visual Representation Viability):} Frozen latent features from a pretrained visual representation with a compact temporal classifier provide a viable video-only gesture baseline.
    \item \textbf{H2 (Kinematic Information):} Lightweight temporal sequence modeling over normalized 76-D kinematic variables yields strong gesture discrimination well above trivial majority baselines.
    \item \textbf{H3 (Multimodal Complementarity):} Multimodal fusion outperforms the strongest unimodal baseline under strictly identical, leakage-safe splits.
    \item \textbf{H4 (Reliability-Aware Gating):} Dynamic confidence-weighted gating maintains higher gesture recognition accuracy under controlled synthetic corruptions and missingness than fixed fusion.
    \item \textbf{H5 (Temporal Sequence Modeling):} Explicit temporal context (GRU) over windowed sequences significantly reduces temporal fragmentation and improves sequence edit score compared to frame-independent predictions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Contributions}
The primary contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unified Multi-Task Benchmark:} A leakage-safe data pipeline processing all 103 trials across all three JIGSAWS tasks (Suturing, Knot Tying, Needle Passing) evaluated via official Leave-One-User-Out (LOUO) cross-validation (8 active folds for Suturing and Knot Tying; 7 active folds for Needle Passing due to an empty test set in the official distribution for Subject G).
    \item \textbf{Audited Multimodal Benchmark Evaluation:} Empirical results comparing Majority Baseline, ResNet-18 VideoGRU, SurgMotion VideoMLP, SurgMotion VideoGRU, Kinematics GRU, Fixed Late Fusion, Feature Concatenation, and Adaptive Reliability Gating across all 103 trials.
    \item \textbf{Statistical Significance \& Effect Size Analysis:} Paired fold-level hypothesis testing with step-down Holm-Bonferroni correction and Cohen's $d_z$ effect sizes, identifying where multimodal gains reach family-wise significance versus where they represent descriptive trends.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Dynamic Gating with Single-Modality Fallback:} A lightweight gating architecture receiving explicit availability indicators and representation statistics that dynamically weights modality streams, achieving top accuracy across all three tasks and strict single-modality fallback under sensor failure.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Robustness Stress Testing:} Standardized evaluation across clean, visual corruption, kinematics corruption, and single-modality dropout conditions, demonstrating that adaptive reliability gating mitigates degradation collapse.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Surgical Workflow and Gesture Recognition}
Since the introduction of the JHU-ISI Gesture and Skill Assessment Working Set (JIGSAWS) by Gao et al.~\cite{gao2014jigsaws}, gesture recognition has evolved from Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) and linear dynamical systems to deep convolutional networks, Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs), and Vision Transformers~\cite{twinanda2016endonet}. While early works focused on unimodal kinematics or raw RGB frames, recent efforts emphasize multimodal fusion.

\subsection{Multimodal Vision-Kinematics Modeling}
Recent frameworks have highlighted the synergy between visual and kinematic channels. Bai et al.~\cite{bai2025grad} introduced GRAD, employing graph representation networks and adversarial feature disentanglement across spatial, wavelet, and Fourier domains on MISAW and CUHK-MRG datasets. Concurrently, Zhou et al.~\cite{zhou2026surgsync} demonstrated that millisecond-level timestamp synchronization is essential to avoid temporal phase lag between kinematic states and video frames on the da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK).

\subsection{Video Foundation Models and Robustness}
Foundation models have increasingly complemented generic ImageNet-pretrained backbones in surgical video analysis. SurgMotion~\cite{wu2026surgmotion} introduced a motion-guided latent prediction objective using Video-JEPA across 3,658 hours of multi-organ surgical procedures, demonstrating that motion saliency captures surgical dynamics. In parallel, Rashid et al.~\cite{rashid2025robustendoclip} proposed the Endo-C6 benchmark, demonstrating that deep vision models suffer severe collapse under clinical artifacts such as cautery smoke, lens fog, and packet loss.

\subsection{76-D Kinematic Feature Representation}
For every sampled timestamp $t$ (sampled at 30\,Hz), the kinematic vector $\mathbf{k}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{76}$ encompasses four robotic manipulators:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{k}_t = \left[ \mathbf{m}_{t}^{\text{left}}, \mathbf{m}_{t}^{\text{right}}, \mathbf{s}_{t}^{\text{left}}, \mathbf{s}_{t}^{\text{right}} \right]
\end{equation}
where each manipulator vector $\mathbf{m}_t, \mathbf{s}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{19}$ consists of:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Tool-tip Cartesian coordinates $\mathbf{p} = [x, y, z]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^3$,
    \item Rotation matrix orientation $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^9$,
    \item Linear translational velocities $\mathbf{v} = [\dot{x}, \dot{y}, \dot{z}]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^3$,
    \item Rotational angular velocities $\boldsymbol{\omega} \in \mathbb{R}^3$,
    \item Gripper jaw angle $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^1$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Leakage-Safe Normalization \& Split Protocol}
To strictly protect against data leakage across training and testing splits:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{k}}_t = \frac{\mathbf{k}_t - \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{train}}}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{train}} + \epsilon}
\end{equation}
where normalization statistics $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{train}}$ and $\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{train}}$ are computed \textit{exclusively} over the training trials of each fold. Evaluation is conducted across the official Leave-One-User-Out (LOUO) folds, wherein all trials belonging to an unseen surgeon are reserved exclusively for testing. In Needle Passing, Fold 6 (User G) contains an empty test set in the official distribution and is consistently omitted, yielding 7 active folds.

\section{Proposed Methodology}

\subsection{Visual Representation via Frozen Embeddings}
We evaluate visual features from both ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 ($D_v=512$)~\cite{he2016deep} and the self-supervised surgical foundation model SurgMotion-ViTL ($D_v=1024$)~\cite{wu2026surgmotion}. For SurgMotion, video clips of 64 frames with stride 16 are processed through the frozen motion-guided transformer encoder:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_t^{\text{vis}} = \Phi_{\text{SurgMotion}}(V_{t-32:t+31}) \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}
\end{equation}
Each tubelet is mapped to gesture annotations and kinematics using the clip center frame $c_t = (s_t + e_t) // 2$. Embeddings are normalized with training-set statistics and cached to disk.

\subsection{Temporal Sequence Modeling (GRU)}
To model temporal dynamics over a context window of length $S=8$, normalized kinematic sequences $\mathbf{K}_{1:S} = (\hat{\mathbf{k}}_1, \dots, \hat{\mathbf{k}}_S)$ and visual feature sequences $\mathbf{Z}_{1:S}^{\text{vis}} = (\hat{\mathbf{z}}_1^{\text{vis}}, \dots, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_S^{\text{vis}})$ are processed through modality-specific recurrent encoders:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_t^m = \text{GRU}_m\!\left(\text{Proj}_m(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t^m),\, \mathbf{h}_{t-1}^m\right), \quad m \in \{\text{kin}, \text{vis}\}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t^{\text{kin}} = \hat{\mathbf{k}}_t$, $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t^{\text{vis}} = \hat{\mathbf{z}}_t^{\text{vis}}$, $\text{Proj}_{\text{vis}}$ projects $1024$-D visual features to dimension $d=128$, and $\text{Proj}_{\text{kin}}$ maps $76$-D kinematics to dimension $d=128$ with LayerNorm, GELU, and Dropout ($p=0.2$). Recurrent hidden states $\mathbf{h}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$ are mapped to gesture class logits via causal linear classifiers $\mathbf{y}_t^{\text{vis}}, \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{kin}} \in \mathbb{R}^C$.

\subsection{Fixed Multimodal Fusion Baselines}
\textbf{Late-Logit Fusion:} Modality-specific classifiers produce independent logits $\mathbf{y}_t^{\text{vis}}, \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{kin}} \in \mathbb{R}^C$. Fixed fusion combines these via convex weighting:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{y}_t^{\text{late}} = \alpha \cdot \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{vis}} + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{kin}}, \quad \alpha \in [0, 1]
\end{equation}
where $\alpha$ is selected via grid search on training-set validation splits, strictly isolating test data.

\textbf{Feature Concatenation Fusion:} Synchronized visual feature vectors $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_t^{\text{vis}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$ and kinematics vectors $\hat{\mathbf{k}}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{76}$ are concatenated at each timestep:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_t^{\text{cat}} = [\hat{\mathbf{z}}_t^{\text{vis}}; \hat{\mathbf{k}}_t] \in \mathbb{R}^{1100}
\end{equation}
The concatenated representations are projected into dimension $d=128$ and modeled via a 2-layer GRU sequence encoder $\mathbf{h}_t^{\text{cat}} = \text{GRU}_{\text{cat}}(\text{Proj}_{\text{cat}}(\mathbf{x}_t^{\text{cat}}), \mathbf{h}_{t-1}^{\text{cat}})$.

\subsection{Reliability-Aware Dynamic Gating}
Under clinical operation, visual or kinematic streams may become corrupted or completely unavailable. We introduce a gating network $G(\cdot)$ that receives projected sequence representations alongside an explicit binary availability vector $\mathbf{a}_t = [a_t^{\text{vis}}, a_t^{\text{kin}}]^\top \in \{0, 1\}^2$:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_t = \text{Softplus}\left( \mathbf{W}_g [\mathbf{h}_t^{\text{vis}}; \mathbf{h}_t^{\text{kin}}; \mathbf{a}_t] + \mathbf{b}_g \right)
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_t = [\tilde{\alpha}_t^{\text{vis}}, \tilde{\alpha}_t^{\text{kin}}]^\top$. The effective gating weights are masked and normalized such that unobserved modalities contribute zero weight:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_t^m = \frac{\tilde{\alpha}_t^m \, a_t^m}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{M}} \tilde{\alpha}_t^j \, a_t^j + \epsilon}, \quad m \in \mathcal{M}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{M} = \{\text{vis}, \text{kin}\}$.
The fused classification logit is then computed as:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{y}}_t = \alpha_t^{\text{vis}} \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{vis}} + \alpha_t^{\text{kin}} \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{kin}}
\end{equation}
When $a_t^{\text{vis}} = 0$ and $a_t^{\text{kin}} = 1$, the gate enforces $\alpha_t^{\text{vis}} \equiv 0$ and $\alpha_t^{\text{kin}} \equiv 1$, guaranteeing exact single-modality fallback $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_t = \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{kin}}$ without numerical drift. Conversely, when $a_t^{\text{vis}} = 1$ and $a_t^{\text{kin}} = 0$, $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_t = \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{vis}}$. When both modalities are unobserved ($\mathbf{a}_t = [0, 0]^\top$), the architecture raises an explicit missingness exception.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{H1 (Visual Representation Viability):} Supported with task-dependent caveats. Pretrained visual representations (SurgMotion VideoGRU and ResNet-18 VideoGRU) provide viable gesture discrimination well above chance in class-balanced metrics, outperforming trivial majority baselines in Balanced Accuracy ($31.0\%$--$54.5\%$ vs. $10.3\%$--$14.9\%$) and Macro-F1 ($20.0\%$--$45.5\%$ vs. $4.6\%$--$6.1\%$) across all three tasks. In raw frame accuracy, visual representations outperform the majority baseline on Suturing ($39.17\%$ vs. $33.43\%$) and Knot Tying ($53.60\%$ vs. $27.35\%$), but fall below the majority baseline on Needle Passing ($26.55\%$ vs. $40.55\%$), where extreme class skew favors trivially predicting the dominant class. Thus, while visual foundation features carry genuine discriminative context, video alone is not a universally reliable standalone predictor across imbalanced tasks.
    \item \textbf{H2 (Kinematics Predictive Power):} Supported. Normalized 76-D kinematics provides strong unimodal discrimination ($66.64\%$ accuracy on Suturing, $61.09\%$ on Knot Tying, $48.69\%$ on Needle Passing), beating trivial majority baselines by $+8.14\%$ to $+33.74\%$ absolute points. Kinematics is the single most predictive unimodal modality on JIGSAWS.
    \item \textbf{H3 (Multimodal Complementarity):} Partially supported. Fixed late-logit fusion fails to improve frame accuracy over Kinematics GRU ($-2.14\%$ to $-12.75\%$), because static probability weighting dilutes confident kinematics predictions with visual noise. Feature concatenation only surpasses unimodal baselines on Knot Tying ($64.09\%$ vs. $61.09\%$, $+3.00\%$), while suffering on Suturing ($54.00\%$ vs. $66.64\%$, $-12.64\%$) due to dimension imbalance ($1024$-D vs. $76$-D). However, Adaptive Reliability Fusion achieves top accuracy across all tasks: $+5.64\%$ on Suturing, $+11.25\%$ on Knot Tying, and $+2.79\%$ on Needle Passing.
    \item \textbf{H4 (Reliability-Aware Gating \& Robustness):} Supported. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:statistical_effects}, paired fold-level tests confirm that Adaptive Fusion achieves statistically significant improvements on Knot Tying across Accuracy ($+11.25\%$, $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0286$, $d_z=1.25$), Balanced Accuracy ($+10.20\%$, $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0286$, $d_z=1.23$), Macro-F1 ($+8.73\%$, $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0457$, $d_z=0.86$), and Edit Score ($+29.18\%$, $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0004$, $d_z=2.79$). On Suturing, accuracy gains ($+5.64\%$) represent a strong trend ($t=2.78$, $p=0.0273$, $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0820$, $d_z=0.98$). On Needle Passing ($N=7$), accuracy gains ($+2.79\%$) represent descriptive trends ($t=0.70$, $p=0.510$).
    \item \textbf{H5 (Temporal Sequence Modeling):} Strongly supported. Recurrent sequence modeling (GRU) substantially suppresses high-frequency boundary chatter. As illustrated in the real held-out trial ribbon timeline (Fig.~\ref{fig:timelines}), Adaptive Fusion nearly doubles the Edit Score over unimodal kinematics ($+23.46\%$ to $+34.26\%$ absolute points across all tasks, $p < 0.001$, $d_z > 2.7$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Controlled Ablation and Temporal Dynamics Analysis}
To dissect the architectural drivers of multimodal performance, we evaluated controlled ablations across all 23 active LOUO folds:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sequence Quality vs. Frame Classification:} Comparing framewise kinematic MLPs with recurrent sequence GRUs reveals that temporal sequence modeling produces negligible shifts in frame accuracy ($\Delta \le 1.26\%$, all $p > 0.40$), but drives an overwhelming, universally significant improvement in segmental Edit Score ($+22.68$ to $+26.25$ points for unimodal GRU, and $+44.69$ to $+59.17$ points for Multimodal Adaptive GRU; all $p < 0.0005, d_z > 2.2$). Temporal recurrence suppresses high-frequency frame chatter, establishing contiguous, clinically coherent gesture segments.
    \item \textbf{Static Gate vs. Temporal Variation:} Replacing the time-varying gate weight $\alpha_t$ with the static fold/trial mean $\bar{\alpha}$ shifted clean accuracy by less than $0.06\%$ across all tasks. Under clean conditions, the gate operates primarily as an availability router and global modality balancer rather than a high-frequency temporal filter.
    \item \textbf{Rare-Gesture Collapse:} Across all tasks, auxiliary and corrective gestures comprising $<3\%$ of the dataset (e.g., G9 and G10 in Suturing; G8 and G9 in Needle Passing) suffer severe recall collapse ($F1 < 6.0\%$), explaining why Macro-F1 lags overall frame accuracy and highlighting class-balanced loss functions as an important direction for surgical activity recognition.
\end{enumerate}

Inverse-frequency Class-Weighted CE trended numerically higher in Balanced Accuracy ($60.05 \pm 12.23\%$, $+2.32\%$ over CE) and Segmental Edit Score ($68.95 \pm 10.03\%$, $+3.58\%$ over CE); however, neither difference reaches statistical significance after family-wise Holm-Bonferroni correction ($p_{\text{adj}} = 1.000$ and $p_{\text{adj}} = 0.2634$ respectively, $m=3$ comparisons against the Standard CE control). To assess whether the Balanced Accuracy difference reflects true recovery of rare gestures, we conducted an empirical frame-level prediction audit across all 32 cross-validation test runs. This analysis demonstrates severe feature starvation on extreme tail gestures: for G10 (loosening suture, 91 frames, 1.1\% of Suturing), recall was strictly $0.00\%$ ($F_1 = 0.00\%$) across all four loss formulations, with zero true positive frames predicted. For G9 (dropping suture, 140 frames, 2.5\%), Standard CE and Focal Loss also collapsed to $0.00\%$ recall, while Class-Weighted CE yielded only $6.43\%$ recall (9 true positive frames out of 140, 58 false positives, precision $13.43\%$, $F_1 = 8.70\%$) and CB-Focal attained $4.29\%$ recall ($F_1 = 7.27\%$). The $+2.32\%$ Balanced Accuracy lift was primarily driven by moderate-frequency gestures—notably G5 (moving to center), whose recall increased by $+21.88\%$ (from $33.33\%$ to $55.21\%$), and G1 (reaching needle, $+6.25\%$). This rebalancing came at the direct expense of the dominant gesture G3 (making C-loop, $30.6\%$ of data), whose recall decreased by $-6.95\%$ (from $85.08\%$ to $78.13\%$), accounting for the overall raw accuracy drop. Furthermore, subject-level analysis suggests that while experienced surgeons (e.g., Users C, F, H) show numerical improvements under class weighting, outlier cases such as User G reflect surgeon-specific kinematic variation and domain shift rather than sample frequency alone.

\section{Discussion \& Limitations}
Our empirical findings demonstrate that lightweight multimodal fusion and adaptive reliability gating provide substantial advantages for robotic surgical gesture recognition. By decoupling visual perception into frozen representations and training compact temporal sequence models, the complete cross-validation pipeline operates efficiently while preventing overfitting.

\textbf{Dataset Size \& Statistical Uncertainty:} JIGSAWS contains 103 trials across 8 subjects. In Leave-One-User-Out cross-validation, the effective sample size for hypothesis testing is $N=8$ (and $N=7$ for Needle Passing). While Knot Tying achieves statistical significance across all metrics ($p_{\text{adj}} \le 0.0457$), Suturing ($p_{\text{adj}}=0.0820$) and Needle Passing represent descriptive trends under conservative family-wise error rate control. Small sample sizes also reduce the power of non-parametric rank tests.

\textbf{Hardware Synchronization Approximation:} In JIGSAWS, video capture (30\,FPS) and robot kinematics (nominal 30\,Hz) were initiated via independent software commands without hardware genlock synchronization. While our audit confirmed a median length difference of $5.0$ frames ($\sim 0.17$\,s), recording tail offsets average $12.37$ frames (and up to $470$ frames tail shutoff lag). Temporal alignment is a discrete frame-center approximation within $\pm 5$ to $15$ frames ($\sim 167$ to $500$\,ms). Hardware master-clock synchronization (e.g., SurgSync~\cite{zhou2026surgsync}) is required for microsecond alignment.

\textbf{Synthetic vs. Real-World Corruptions:} The robustness experiments evaluate controlled Gaussian perturbations and discrete missingness masks. While informative for testing gate fallback guarantees, they remain synthetic approximations of complex clinical disturbances such as blood splatter, lens condensation, or robotic cable hysteresis.

\textbf{IEEE Transactions Compliance \& Submission Considerations:} While this manuscript is prepared using the standard IEEEtran template, submission to journals such as the IEEE Transactions on Medical Robotics and Bionics (T-MRB) or IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering (TBME) imposes specific structural criteria: (i) Regular papers are budgeted for 8 published double-column pages, with mandatory overlength page charges assessed beyond page 8; (ii) Official peer review accepts double-column preprints for rapid evaluation or single-column double-spaced review drafts; (iii) JIGSAWS evaluations use de-identified publicly accessible benchtop data, satisfying institutional human-subjects research exemptions; (iv) Final journal submission requires multimedia accompaniment (e.g., gesture timeline video demonstrations) and full artifact archives for algorithmic reproducibility.
 
\section{Conclusion}
We presented a rigorous empirical audit and benchmark for multimodal surgical gesture recognition evaluated across all 103 trials of the JIGSAWS benchmark under official Leave-One-User-Out cross-validation. We validated the viability of frozen visual representations (H1), confirmed the primary predictive utility of normalized robot kinematics (H2), demonstrated that adaptive reliability gating outperforms unimodal baselines while simple late fusion degrades accuracy (H3), demonstrated that availability-masked dynamic gating provides seamless single-modality fallback under sensor failure (H4), and established that recurrent temporal modeling dramatically reduces oversegmentation chatter (H5). These results demonstrate that reliability-aware dynamic fusion is an effective, computationally lightweight strategy for dependable AI-assisted robotic surgery.
