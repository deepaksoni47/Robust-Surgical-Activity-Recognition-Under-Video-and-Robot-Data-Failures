\begin{abstract}
Automated surgical gesture recognition is a fundamental prerequisite for context-aware assistance, objective skill assessment, and autonomous execution in robot-assisted minimally invasive surgery (RMIS). While endoscopic video conveys semantic context of tool-tissue interactions, robot kinematics provides continuous physical trajectories immune to optical degradation. However, conventional multimodal models frequently rely on static concatenation or uncalibrated late fusion, leaving them vulnerable when visual corruptions (smoke, fog, blur) or telemetry dropouts occur. In this work, we present an empirical benchmark of an adaptive multimodal framework evaluated across all 103 trials of the JIGSAWS benchmark: Suturing (39 trials), Knot Tying (36 trials), and Needle Passing (28 trials). We combine frozen surgical foundation model features (SurgMotion-ViTL, 1024-D) and ImageNet ResNet-18 embeddings (512-D) with normalized 76-D da Vinci kinematics. Lightweight temporal sequence models (GRU) capture temporal dynamics, while a reliability-aware dynamic gating network calibrates modality confidence from representation statistics and explicit availability flags to enforce exact single-modality fallback under sensor failure. Under official Leave-One-User-Out (LOUO) cross-validation across all active folds (8 folds for Suturing and Knot Tying; 7 active folds for Needle Passing due to an empty test set for Subject G in the official distribution), empirical evaluations demonstrate: (i) Normalized 76-D kinematics provides strong unimodal gesture discrimination ($66.64\%$ accuracy on Suturing, $61.09\%$ on Knot Tying, $48.69\%$ on Needle Passing); (ii) Simple fixed late fusion degrades frame accuracy ($-2.14\%$ to $-12.75\%$) by allowing visual noise to dilute confident kinematics predictions; (iii) Adaptive Reliability Gating achieves the highest accuracy across all three tasks ($72.28\%$ on Suturing, $72.34\%$ on Knot Tying, and $51.48\%$ on Needle Passing), demonstrating a statistically significant $+11.25\%$ gain on Knot Tying ($p=0.0095$, Holm-Bonferroni $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0286$, Cohen's $d_z=1.25$); (iv) Multimodal temporal modeling substantially stabilizes sequence coherence, producing large and statistically significant improvements in segmental Edit Score ($+23.46\%$ to $+34.26\%$, $p < 0.001$, effect sizes $d_z > 2.7$); and (v) Under complete optical telemetry failure, the adaptive gate dynamically routes prediction mass to kinematics, preserving performance with minimal degradation ($\le 0.42\%$ drop on Suturing and Needle Passing).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{R}{obotic} minimally invasive surgery (RMIS), exemplified by the da Vinci Surgical System, has revolutionized surgical interventions through high-definition 3D stereoscopic vision, tremor filtration, and wristed articulating instruments~\cite{gao2014jigsaws, maierhein2022surgical}. To enable context-aware intraoperative assistance, supervisory autonomy, and objective skill credentialing, automated systems must parse continuous surgical workflows into discrete atomic gestures (e.g., reaching, needle passing, suture tying) in real time~\cite{ahmidi2017dataset, dipietro2016recognizing}.

Surgical perception relies on two complementary sensory streams:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Endoscopic Video:} Captures rich photometric tool-tissue interactions, tissue elasticity, suture threading, and anatomical boundaries. However, optical endoscopy in real clinical theaters is routinely corrupted by cautery smoke, lens fogging, blood splatter, illumination shifts, and digital transmission artifacts~\cite{rashid2025robustendoclip}.
    \item \textbf{Robot Kinematics:} High-frequency telemetry comprising tool-tip Cartesian coordinates, 3D rotation matrices, linear and angular velocities, and gripper closure states. While kinematics is completely invariant to optical degradation, it lacks direct information regarding environmental geometry, tissue contact resistance, and suture tension~\cite{bai2025grad, zhou2026surgsync, vanamsterdam2021gesture}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Research Question \& Hypotheses}
The central question governing this investigation is:
\begin{quote}
\textit{On JIGSAWS surgical gesture recognition, does lightweight temporal fusion of pretrained video representations and robot kinematics outperform unimodal baselines, and does reliability-aware dynamic gating reduce performance loss when one modality is degraded or unavailable?}
\end{quote}

We evaluate five specific hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{H1 (Visual Representation Viability):} Frozen latent features from a pretrained visual foundation model with a compact temporal head provide a viable standalone video baseline.
    \item \textbf{H2 (Kinematic Predictive Power):} Lightweight temporal sequence modeling over normalized 76-D kinematics yields strong gesture discrimination well above trivial majority baselines.
    \item \textbf{H3 (Multimodal Complementarity):} Multimodal fusion outperforms the strongest unimodal baseline under strictly identical, leakage-safe splits.
    \item \textbf{H4 (Reliability-Aware Gating):} Dynamic confidence-weighted gating maintains higher gesture recognition accuracy under controlled synthetic corruptions and missingness than fixed fusion.
    \item \textbf{H5 (Temporal Sequence Modeling):} Explicit temporal context (GRU) over windowed sequences significantly reduces temporal fragmentation and improves sequence edit score compared to frame-independent predictions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Contributions}
The primary contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unified Multi-Task Benchmark:} A leakage-safe data pipeline processing all 103 trials across all three JIGSAWS tasks (Suturing, Knot Tying, Needle Passing) evaluated via official Leave-One-User-Out (LOUO) cross-validation across all active folds ($N=8$ Suturing, $N=8$ Knot Tying, $N=7$ Needle Passing, where Subject G is empty in the official distribution).
    \item \textbf{Audited Multimodal Comparative Evaluation:} Empirical evaluation across all 103 trials comparing Majority Baseline, ResNet-18 VideoGRU, SurgMotion VideoMLP, SurgMotion VideoGRU, Kinematics GRU, Fixed Late Fusion, Feature Concatenation, and Adaptive Reliability Gating.
    \item \textbf{Statistical Rigor \& Effect Size Analysis:} Paired fold-level hypothesis testing with step-down Holm--Bonferroni correction~\cite{holm1979simple} and Cohen's $d_z$ effect sizes~\cite{cohen1988statistical}, establishing where multimodal gains reach family-wise significance versus descriptive trends.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Dynamic Gating with Guaranteed Fallback:} A lightweight gating network receiving explicit availability flags and sequence statistics that dynamically weights modality streams and enforces exact single-modality mathematical fallback under sensor collapse.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Robustness Stress Testing:} Standardized evaluation across clean, visual corruption, kinematics corruption, and sensor dropout conditions, demonstrating that adaptive reliability gating mitigates catastrophic performance collapse.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Surgical Activity and Gesture Recognition}
Since the release of the JHU-ISI Gesture and Skill Assessment Working Set (JIGSAWS)~\cite{gao2014jigsaws, ahmidi2017dataset}, surgical activity analysis has evolved from Markovian models to deep spatiotemporal sequence architectures. Early investigations primarily utilized Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) and Linear Dynamical Systems (LDS) over kinematics telemetry~\cite{ahmidi2017dataset}. DiPietro et al.~\cite{dipietro2016recognizing} introduced recurrent neural networks for kinematics-based gesture segmentation, showing that sequential memory outperforms memoryless frame classifiers. Subsequently, Colin Lea et al.~\cite{lea2017temporal} formulated Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs) with dilated causal convolutions, establishing that hierarchically expanding temporal receptive fields capture prolonged gesture durations while avoiding gradient degradation. This was further generalized by Abu Farha and Gall~\cite{farha2019ms} via Multi-Stage TCNs (MS-TCN) and by Funke et al.~\cite{funke2019temporal} and Jin et al.~\cite{jin2020temporal} via recurrent convolutional networks that couple spatial feature extraction with temporal sequence regularization.

\subsection{Multimodal Vision-Kinematics Fusion}
Complementarity between optical vision and robotic kinematics has motivated diverse fusion paradigms. Sarikaya et al.~\cite{sarikaya2017surgical} demonstrated multimodal tool tracking and surgical skill evaluation, while van Amsterdam et al.~\cite{vanamsterdam2021gesture} designed multimodal attention mechanisms that dynamically balance spatial tool features with Cartesian kinematic coordinates. Recently, Bai et al.~\cite{bai2025grad} introduced GRAD, employing multimodal graph representation learning and adversarial feature disentanglement across wavelet and Fourier spectra to decouple style variations from underlying surgical actions. In parallel, Zhou et al.~\cite{zhou2026surgsync} established with SurgSync that physical sensor synchronization is critical, as inter-modality timing discrepancies induce phase shifts that impair joint inference.

\subsection{Surgical Foundation Models and Robustness}
Large-scale foundation models have emerged as powerful visual feature extractors for clinical video understanding. SurgMotion~\cite{wu2026surgmotion} developed a video-native foundation model using Video-JEPA latent motion prediction pretrained on 3,658 hours of multi-organ surgical videos, capturing fine tool-tissue dynamics without task-specific fine-tuning. Concurrently, broader surgical data science roadmaps~\cite{maierhein2022surgical} have emphasized that clinical translation requires algorithms to remain resilient under harsh operating room perturbations. Addressing this vulnerability, Rashid et al.~\cite{rashid2025robustendoclip} introduced the Endo-C6 benchmark, demonstrating that state-of-the-art vision models degrade precipitously when subjected to electrocautery smoke, optical blur, and camera occlusion. These findings establish the necessity of multimodal architectures capable of robust, real-time fallback under sensory degradation.

\subsection{76-D Kinematic Feature Representation}
For every sampled timestamp $t$ (sampled at 30\,Hz), the kinematic vector $\mathbf{k}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{76}$ encompasses four robotic manipulators:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{k}_t = \left[ \mathbf{m}_{t}^{\text{left}}, \mathbf{m}_{t}^{\text{right}}, \mathbf{s}_{t}^{\text{left}}, \mathbf{s}_{t}^{\text{right}} \right]
\end{equation}
where each manipulator vector $\mathbf{m}_t, \mathbf{s}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{19}$ consists of:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Tool-tip Cartesian coordinates $\mathbf{p} = [x, y, z]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^3$,
    \item Rotation matrix orientation $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^9$,
    \item Linear translational velocities $\mathbf{v} = [\dot{x}, \dot{y}, \dot{z}]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^3$,
    \item Rotational angular velocities $\boldsymbol{\omega} \in \mathbb{R}^3$,
    \item Gripper jaw angle $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^1$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Leakage-Safe Normalization \& Split Protocol}
To strictly protect against data leakage across training and testing splits:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{k}}_t = \frac{\mathbf{k}_t - \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{train}}}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{train}} + \epsilon}
\end{equation}
where normalization statistics $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{train}}$ and $\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{train}}$ are computed \textit{exclusively} over the training trials of each fold. Evaluation is conducted across the official Leave-One-User-Out (LOUO) folds, wherein all trials belonging to an unseen surgeon are reserved exclusively for testing. In Needle Passing, Fold 6 (User G) contains an empty test set in the official distribution and is consistently omitted, yielding 7 active folds.

\subsection{Visual Representation via Frozen Embeddings}
We benchmark visual representations from ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 ($D_v=512$)~\cite{he2016deep} and the surgical foundation model SurgMotion-ViTL ($D_v=1024$)~\cite{wu2026surgmotion}. In SurgMotion, 64-frame video clips with stride 16 are processed through the frozen motion-guided transformer encoder:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_t^{\text{vis}} = \Phi_{\text{SurgMotion}}(V_{t-32:t+31}) \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}
\end{equation}
Each tubelet is mapped to gesture annotations and kinematics using the clip center frame $c_t = (s_t + e_t) // 2$. Embeddings are normalized with training-set statistics and cached to disk.

\subsection{Temporal Sequence Modeling (GRU)}
To capture fine-grained temporal context across sliding windows of length $S=8$, normalized kinematic sequences $\mathbf{K}_{1:S} = (\hat{\mathbf{k}}_1, \dots, \hat{\mathbf{k}}_S)$ and visual representations $\mathbf{Z}_{1:S}^{\text{vis}} = (\hat{\mathbf{z}}_1^{\text{vis}}, \dots, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_S^{\text{vis}})$ are first projected into a common latent dimension $d=128$:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_t^m = \text{LayerNorm}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}^m \hat{\mathbf{x}}_t^m + \mathbf{b}_{\text{proj}}^m\right), \quad m \in \{\text{kin}, \text{vis}\}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t^{\text{kin}} = \hat{\mathbf{k}}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{76}$, $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t^{\text{vis}} = \hat{\mathbf{z}}_t^{\text{vis}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$, $\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}^{\text{kin}} \in \mathbb{R}^{128 \times 76}$, and $\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}^{\text{vis}} \in \mathbb{R}^{128 \times 1024}$. Projected vectors are passed through GELU activation with Dropout ($p=0.2$) and modeled by a 2-layer Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU)~\cite{cho2014learning} sequence encoder:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_t^m = \text{GRU}_m\!\left(\text{Dropout}(\text{GELU}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_t^m)),\, \mathbf{h}_{t-1}^m\right)
\end{equation}
The recurrent hidden states $\mathbf{h}_t^m \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$ are mapped to gesture class logits via causal linear classifier heads $\mathbf{y}_t^m = \mathbf{W}_{\text{cls}}^m \mathbf{h}_t^m + \mathbf{b}_{\text{cls}}^m$, where $\mathbf{W}_{\text{cls}}^m \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times 128}$ and $C$ is the gesture vocabulary size.

\subsection{Fixed Multimodal Fusion Baselines}
\textbf{Late-Logit Fusion:} Modality-specific classifiers produce independent logits $\mathbf{y}_t^{\text{vis}}, \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{kin}} \in \mathbb{R}^{C}$. Fixed fusion combines these via convex weighting:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{y}_t^{\text{late}} = \alpha \cdot \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{vis}} + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{kin}}, \quad \alpha \in [0, 1]
\end{equation}
where $\alpha$ is selected via grid search on training-set validation splits, strictly isolating test data.

\textbf{Feature Concatenation Fusion:} Synchronized visual feature vectors $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_t^{\text{vis}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$ and kinematics vectors $\hat{\mathbf{k}}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{76}$ are concatenated at each timestep:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_t^{\text{cat}} = [\hat{\mathbf{z}}_t^{\text{vis}}; \hat{\mathbf{k}}_t] \in \mathbb{R}^{1100}
\end{equation}
The concatenated representations are projected into dimension $d=128$ and modeled via a 2-layer GRU sequence encoder $\mathbf{h}_t^{\text{cat}} = \text{GRU}_{\text{cat}}(\text{Proj}_{\text{cat}}(\mathbf{x}_t^{\text{cat}}), \mathbf{h}_{t-1}^{\text{cat}})$.

\subsection{Reliability-Aware Dynamic Gating}
Under clinical operation, visual or kinematic streams may become corrupted or completely unavailable. We introduce a gating network parameterized by $\mathbf{W}_g \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times (2d+2)}$ and $\mathbf{b}_g \in \mathbb{R}^2$ ($d=128$, input dimension $2d+2=258$). The gate receives concatenated sequence representations alongside an explicit binary sensor availability vector $\mathbf{a}_t = [a_t^{\text{vis}}, a_t^{\text{kin}}]^\top \in \{0, 1\}^2$:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_t = \text{Softplus}\left( \mathbf{W}_g [\mathbf{h}_t^{\text{vis}}; \mathbf{h}_t^{\text{kin}}; \mathbf{a}_t] + \mathbf{b}_g \right)
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_t = [\tilde{\alpha}_t^{\text{vis}}, \tilde{\alpha}_t^{\text{kin}}]^\top$. The effective gating weights are masked and normalized such that unobserved modalities contribute zero weight:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_t^m = \frac{\tilde{\alpha}_t^m \, a_t^m}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{M}} \tilde{\alpha}_t^j \, a_t^j + \epsilon}, \quad m \in \mathcal{M} = \{\text{vis}, \text{kin}\}
\end{equation}
The fused classification logit is computed as $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_t = \alpha_t^{\text{vis}} \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{vis}} + \alpha_t^{\text{kin}} \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{kin}}$.
When $a_t^{\text{vis}} = 0$ and $a_t^{\text{kin}} = 1$, the gate enforces $\alpha_t^{\text{vis}} \equiv 0$ and $\alpha_t^{\text{kin}} \equiv 1$, guaranteeing exact single-modality fallback $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_t = \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{kin}}$ without numerical drift. Conversely, when $a_t^{\text{vis}} = 1$ and $a_t^{\text{kin}} = 0$, $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_t = \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{vis}}$. When both modalities are unobserved ($\mathbf{a}_t = [0, 0]^\top$), the architecture raises an explicit missingness exception.
The full network is trained end-to-end via multi-class cross-entropy loss over frame ground truth labels $\mathbf{y}_t \in \{0, 1\}^C$: $\mathcal{L} = -\sum_{c=1}^C y_{t,c} \log \hat{p}_{t,c}$, where $\hat{p}_{t,c} = \exp(\hat{y}_{t,c}) / \sum_{j=1}^C \exp(\hat{y}_{t,j})$.

\subsection{Benchmark Analysis \& Statistical Testing}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} presents the consolidated empirical cross-validation results across all 103 trials:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{H1 (Visual Representation Viability):} Supported with task-dependent caveats. Pretrained visual representations (SurgMotion VideoGRU and ResNet-18 VideoGRU) provide viable gesture discrimination well above chance in class-balanced metrics, outperforming trivial majority baselines in Balanced Accuracy ($31.0\%$--$54.5\%$ vs. $10.3\%$--$14.9\%$) and Macro-F1 ($20.0\%$--$45.5\%$ vs. $4.6\%$--$6.1\%$) across all three tasks. In raw frame accuracy, visual representations outperform the majority baseline on Suturing ($39.17\%$ vs. $33.43\%$) and Knot Tying ($53.60\%$ vs. $27.35\%$), but fall below the majority baseline on Needle Passing ($26.55\%$ vs. $40.55\%$), where extreme class skew favors trivially predicting the dominant class. Thus, while visual foundation features carry genuine discriminative context, video alone is not a universally reliable standalone predictor across imbalanced tasks.
    \item \textbf{H2 (Kinematics Predictive Power):} Supported. Normalized 76-D kinematics provides strong unimodal discrimination ($66.64\%$ accuracy on Suturing, $61.09\%$ on Knot Tying, $48.69\%$ on Needle Passing), outperforming trivial majority baselines by $+8.14\%$ to $+33.74\%$ absolute points. Telemetry constitutes the primary unimodal predictor on JIGSAWS.
    \item \textbf{H3 (Multimodal Complementarity):} Partially supported. Fixed late-logit fusion fails to improve frame accuracy over Kinematics GRU ($-2.14\%$ to $-12.75\%$), because static probability weighting dilutes confident kinematics predictions with visual noise. Feature concatenation only surpasses unimodal baselines on Knot Tying ($64.09\%$ vs. $61.09\%$, $+3.00\%$), while suffering on Suturing ($54.00\%$ vs. $66.64\%$, $-12.64\%$) due to dimension imbalance ($1024$-D vs. $76$-D). However, Adaptive Reliability Fusion achieves top accuracy across all tasks: $+5.64\%$ on Suturing, $+11.25\%$ on Knot Tying, and $+2.79\%$ on Needle Passing.
    \item \textbf{H4 (Reliability-Aware Gating \& Robustness):} Supported. Paired fold-level tests evaluate significance via step-down Holm--Bonferroni correction~\cite{holm1979simple} and Cohen's $d_z$ effect sizes~\cite{cohen1988statistical} (Fig.~\ref{fig:statistical_effects}). Adaptive Fusion achieves statistically significant gains on Knot Tying across Accuracy ($+11.25\%$, $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0286$, $d_z=1.25$), Balanced Accuracy ($+10.20\%$, $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0286$, $d_z=1.23$), Macro-F1 ($+8.73\%$, $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0457$, $d_z=0.86$), and Edit Score ($+29.18\%$, $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0004$, $d_z=2.79$). On Suturing, accuracy gains ($+5.64\%$) reflect a strong trend ($t=2.78$, $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0820$, $d_z=0.98$). On Needle Passing ($N=7$), gains ($+2.79\%$) show descriptive positive trends ($t=0.70$, $p=0.510$).
    \item \textbf{H5 (Temporal Sequence Modeling):} Strongly supported. Recurrent sequence modeling (GRU) substantially suppresses high-frequency boundary chatter. As illustrated in the real held-out trial ribbon timeline (Fig.~\ref{fig:timelines}), Adaptive Fusion nearly doubles the Edit Score over unimodal kinematics ($+23.46\%$ to $+34.26\%$ absolute points across all tasks, $p < 0.001$, $d_z > 2.7$).
\end{enumerate}

Table~\ref{tab:robustness_results} evaluates model resilience under five standardized evaluation environments:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Optical Noise Resistance \& Gate Dynamics:} Under visual feature corruption ($\sigma_v=1.0$), Adaptive Fusion degradation is practically negligible ($\le 0.12\%$ drop across all tasks). Detailed empirical analysis of over 4~million time-resolved gate weight logs reveals that this stability is driven by strong kinematics dominance ($\bar{\alpha}_k \approx 80\%$--$95\%$) rather than active continuous noise attenuation: the mean video gate weight $\bar{\alpha}_v$ shifts by less than $0.009$ ($<0.9\%$) across continuous noise levels up to $\sigma_v = 2.0$.
    \item \textbf{Flawless Video Dropout Fallback:} Under complete optical telemetry failure ($a=[0, 1]$), the availability mask mathematically routes $100\%$ prediction mass to kinematics, incurring minimal degradation ($-0.42\%$ on Suturing, $-2.28\%$ on Knot Tying, $-0.27\%$ on Needle Passing).
    \item \textbf{Kinematics Degradation Asymmetry:} When kinematics telemetry is corrupted or lost ($a=[1, 0]$), both models experience severe drops ($-26\%$ to $-41\%$). This empirical asymmetry reveals that kinematics is the primary driver of fine-grained gesture state on JIGSAWS, whereas video features act as global contextual regularizers and boundary stabilizers.
    \item \textbf{Concatenation Anomaly:} On Suturing and Needle Passing, zeroing out video features in Concat Fusion actually increased accuracy ($+17.83\%$ and $+7.78\%$). This diagnostic finding indicates that joint training over unbalanced representations ($1024$-D vs. $76$-D) allowed visual noise to interfere with kinematics representations; removing the video vector eliminated this interference.
    \item \textbf{Continuous-Noise Training Ablation:} In our controlled 46-fold ablation study comparing continuous-noise training ($\sigma_v \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1.5)$) against discrete binary dropout, continuous noise did not alter the gate's static weighting dynamic, but provided valuable visual representation regularization, improving standalone video accuracy under total kinematics failure by $+5.49\%$ on Suturing ($34.44\%$ vs. $28.95\%$) and $+8.96\%$ on Needle Passing ($24.58\%$ vs. $15.62\%$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Controlled Ablation and Temporal Dynamics Analysis}
To dissect the architectural drivers of multimodal performance, we evaluated controlled ablations across all 23 active LOUO folds:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sequence Quality vs. Frame Classification:} Comparing framewise kinematic MLPs with recurrent sequence GRUs reveals that temporal sequence modeling produces negligible shifts in frame accuracy ($\Delta \le 1.26\%$, all $p > 0.40$), but drives an overwhelming, universally significant improvement in segmental Edit Score ($+22.68$ to $+26.25$ points for unimodal GRU, and $+44.69$ to $+59.17$ points for Multimodal Adaptive GRU; all $p < 0.0005, d_z > 2.2$). Temporal recurrence suppresses high-frequency frame chatter, establishing contiguous, clinically coherent gesture segments.
    \item \textbf{Static Gate vs. Temporal Variation:} Replacing the time-varying gate weight $\alpha_t$ with the static fold/trial mean $\bar{\alpha}$ shifted clean accuracy by less than $0.06\%$ across all tasks. Under clean conditions, the gate operates primarily as an availability router and global modality balancer rather than a high-frequency temporal filter.
    \item \textbf{Rare-Gesture Collapse:} Across all tasks, auxiliary and corrective gestures comprising $<3\%$ of the dataset (e.g., G9 and G10 in Suturing; G8 and G9 in Needle Passing) suffer severe recall collapse ($F1 < 6.0\%$), explaining why Macro-F1 lags overall frame accuracy and highlighting class-balanced loss functions as an important direction for surgical activity recognition.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion \& Limitations}
Decoupling visual perception into frozen representations with compact temporal heads enables efficient, leakage-safe cross-validation. Moreover, time-resolved gating weights $\alpha_t^{\text{kin}}, \alpha_t^{\text{vis}}$ serve as an intrinsic clinical proxy of sensor stream integrity without auxiliary uncertainty networks.

\textbf{Statistical Power under LOUO Splits:} Across 103 JIGSAWS trials, Leave-One-User-Out cross-validation enforces strict clinician generalization ($N=8$ subjects for Suturing/Knot Tying, $N=7$ for Needle Passing). Knot Tying achieves statistical significance across all metrics ($p_{\text{adj}} \le 0.0457$); Suturing exhibits a strong directional trend ($p_{\text{adj}}=0.0820, d_z=0.98$), while Needle Passing shows descriptive gains ($+2.79\%$) with higher cross-user variance.

\textbf{Temporal Synchronization:} In systems lacking hardware genlock, clock drift induces small phase offsets (median $5.0$ frames, $\sim 0.17$\,s on JIGSAWS). Master-clock synchronization frameworks (e.g., SurgSync~\cite{zhou2026surgsync}) represent the target operational standard for future multimodal operating theaters.

\textbf{Clinical Disturbances:} Evaluated Gaussian noise and dropout masks model operational disturbances (lens fogging, electrocautery smoke, telemetry drops)~\cite{rashid2025robustendoclip}. In-vivo evaluation will further assess spontaneous bleeding and sudden camera repositioning.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a rigorous multimodal benchmark for surgical gesture recognition across all 103 JIGSAWS trials under official Leave-One-User-Out cross-validation. We confirmed the predictive primacy of normalized kinematics (H2) and the contextual value of frozen visual representations (H1). While static fusion degrades accuracy, adaptive reliability gating dynamically blends representations and enforces exact single-modality fallback under sensor loss (H4), achieving top accuracy across all three tasks. Recurrent sequence modeling suppresses oversegmentation chatter, doubling segmental Edit Scores (H5). These results demonstrate that reliability-aware dynamic fusion provides a lightweight, dependable architecture for context-aware robotic surgery.
